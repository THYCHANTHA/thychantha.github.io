Cambodia Rice-Yield Forecasting

Sub-national rice-yield forecasting built during my research internship at the National Institute of Statistics, comparing gradient-boosting and ensemble models and reporting results in Power BI.

Open full page Read the thesis View on GitHub

Problem

Agricultural planning needs reliable yield estimates below the national level. The goal was to test which predictive models forecast rice yield (kg/ha) most accurately and to present the findings clearly to decision makers.

Overview

Research project completed as a Research and Data Science Intern at the National Institute of Statistics, Ministry of Planning (14 Jul - 30 Sep 2025). It forecasts sub-national rice yields and analyses cultivation to support agricultural planning, and was written up as a thesis on agricultural forecasting.

Methodology

  • Prepared the sub-national cultivation data with Python
  • Trained and compared four regressors: LightGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest, and Gradient Boosting
  • Evaluated models with R², RMSE, and MAE
  • Inspected predicted-vs-actual values, error distribution, and residuals for the selected model
  • Presented results in Power BI dashboards

Key features

  • Model comparison across four algorithms
  • Predicted vs actual yield analysis with a ±10% error band
  • Error-distribution and residual diagnostics
  • Power BI dashboards for cultivation analysis

Technologies

Results

  • R² of 0.904 for LightGBM and XGBoost (Random Forest 0.766, Gradient Boosting 0.855)
  • On the evaluation dashboard: RMSE of 385 kg/ha, with 86.8% of predictions within 10% of the actual yield

LightGBM and XGBoost were the strongest models, each reaching R² = 0.904 in the model comparison. Figures are taken from the evaluation charts shown below; see the full thesis for the complete analysis. Caveat from my own later review: the yield model was evaluated on random parcel-level splits from a single survey year and one feature (storage_ratio) was flagged for possible target leakage, so R-squared 0.904 is an optimistic figure and not a measure of how the model would generalise to new provinces or seasons. A leakage-free production model reached R-squared 0.838.
